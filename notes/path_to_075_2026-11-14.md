Question from the owner: examine the work carefully, consider a different architecture or the ideas of the best
public notebook, and say how 0.75 can be reached. Sources: the leaderboard and public notebooks pulled with the
Kaggle CLI on 14 Nov 12:42 UTC (source only, never run), the forum read the same day, public dataset listings, and

## What changed in public since 12 Nov

- **The public best is now 0.694: the 0.684 stack plus likelihood under the real GLM-5.3 and GLM-5.3-Flash**
  (José Padilla, `estopadilla/0-689-real-glm-5-3-likelihood-on-2xt4`; rerun by alexchilton at 0.694; variants by
  sanpier and lightningv08 at 0.693-0.694). It is the idea of our Exp 10, finished and run on Kaggle's two T4s:
  a patched llama.cpp runs the public layer segments one at a time on the 15-30% of test texts the stack ranks
  highest for the two GLM labels, and a logistic regression on 24 features per model plus 6 differences is added
  to the stack's log-probabilities on those texts.
- **Their numbers.** Out of fold on a proxy base: GLM-5.3 alone +0.004, the Flash − GLM-5.3 contrast +0.035; recall
  glm-5.3 0.27 → 0.63 and glm-5.3-flash 0.45 → 0.65, other labels ±0.02. On the board: +0.007 (0.682 → 0.689) on
  the author's account, 0.684 → 0.694 on alexchilton's. Cost on two T4s: 0.011 s (Flash) and 0.020 s (GLM-5.3) per
  token row plus 23 and 30 minutes of loading; the stack itself takes about 6 h of the 9 h limit.
- **Kimi K3 is next.** Layer slices have been public since 29 Oct (`lianmatsuo/kimik3-iq2xxs-slices-1..4`, 730 GB,
  1,356-2,402 downloads each; their author is 12th at 0.747). A Q2_K_XL upload (13 Nov) and toy GGUFs of the
  architecture for testing a runner (14 Nov) appeared this week. No public notebook runs K3 yet.
  **Costed the same day:** K3 can run on two T4s, but not beside the current pipeline. The slices are 93
  single-layer files of 6.9-10.4 GB, so one layer fits on a 16 GB T4 and the model streams layer by layer as
  GLM-5.3 does. Its config (`moonshotai/Kimi-K3`: 93 layers, hidden 7168, 896 experts per layer, 16 per token)
  and GLM-5.3's published timings (278 GB, 78 layers: 30 min of loading, 0.020 s per token row) scale to about
  80 min of loading and 0.025-0.05 s per token row, so 2 to 2.7 h for 600 texts of 160 tokens. That is an
  estimate; nobody has published a measurement. The stack (about 6 h) and the GLM stage (1-2 h) already fill the
  9 h limit, the files are quantised to about 2 bits per weight (GLM's are 3), and no one has published what K3
  adds.
- **Forum, 14 Nov (discussion 759187):** a team above 0.73 says it submitted only a few times and barely used
  external datasets. The board agrees that volume of submissions is not what the top has: first place has 15
  submissions, 14th (0.746) has 3.
- **Public A/B lessons** (alexchilton's and prvsiyan's tables): one more LoRA member moves a strong stack by at most
  0.004; a member that gained +0.003 to +0.007 on a train-side stand-in lost 0.011 to 0.014 on the board; identical
  code varies by up to 0.009; the public board scores about 40% of the 4,000 passages, so 0.001 is one or two
  passages.

## What our own predictions say (measured today)

**1. The three open-weight labels hold more of our error than any closed family.** Exp 4d's 8B, both partitions:

| Scenario | p0 | p1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the 8B as it is | 0.623 | 0.624 |
| glm-5.3 and glm-5.3-flash identified perfectly (their rows right, no other row given to them) | 0.714 | 0.712 |
| the same with kimi-k3 | **0.752** | **0.752** |
| right sibling whenever the Gemini family is right | 0.697 | 0.697 |
| right sibling whenever the GPT family is right | 0.699 | 0.700 |
| right sibling whenever the Claude family is right | 0.672 | 0.672 |

Perfect identification of the three models whose weights are public would by itself take our single 8B to 0.75.
The public GLM stage captures about a third of the GLM part (+0.03 of +0.09 out of fold), and less on the board.
The rest of that block is GLM against Claude (glm-5.3 → claude-fable-5 50 rows, → other Claude 53; fable → GLM 63),
which likelihood does not separate: Claude text is as likely to GLM as its own (Exp 10: AUC 0.43 between glm-5.3
and claude-fable-5 under Flash; the public finding that GLM-5.3's own likelihood adds +0.004).

**2. Official rows still pay, on the 8B.** A learning curve read from saved files (inner fits train on 64 prompts,
outer fits on 96; stage 1 alone is the zero point):

| Official prompts in stage 2 | 0 | 64 | 96 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Qwen3-8B LoRA24 (p0 / p1) | 0.588 / 0.588 | 0.613 / 0.613 | 0.623 / 0.624 |
| Qwen3-14B LoRA24 (p0 / p1) | 0.588 / 0.588 | 0.615 / 0.618 | 0.609 / 0.621 |

The 8B gains +0.011 for 1.5 times the prompts, about +0.018 per doubling (the 64-prompt figure is at the epoch
selected on those same rows, so the true slope is a little higher). The 14B is flat within its noise. On the 8B's
slope, four times as many official-like prompts would be worth about +0.035 on a single model, which is the size of
the gap between public members (0.62-0.65) and the single models top teams report (0.65-0.68, "nearly all from
data"). Pool rows pay too, but by the hundred thousand: 36k → 212k rows was worth +0.073 (Exp 4d), about +0.028 per
doubling, and the last doubling there is 106k rows against 1.7k official rows. At the margin one official row is
worth about 40 pool rows.

**3. Two ideas checked and dropped.**
- *Panel-balanced stage 1.* The pool is already panel-rich: 78% of its rows answer prompts that six or more labels
  answer, and 69-99% of each multi-label family's rows have a sibling on the same prompt (claude-fable-5 lowest).
  Little to gain.
- *The Kaggle Benchmarks proxy as "the official route".* 37% of the pool (69,745 rows) came through it. On our 4B
  closeness instrument those cells look as official as other routes and no more (median ratio 0.73 against 0.73;
  166 and 134 cells), so the proxy at default settings brings no visible advantage. A cell's ratio also depends on
  its prompts, so this does not prove the host used another route. Kimi sits lowest on every route (0.15-0.27),
  then the sibling-hard labels (gemini-3.8-flash 0.59-0.67, gpt-5.6-sol 0.47-0.62, claude-opus-4-8 0.66-0.68).

## Architecture options, with the evidence for each

| Option | Evidence | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Another backbone or pooling for our own member (Qwen3-Embedding-8B at full depth, 27B, 14B) | These are members of the public stack already; our 8B added to it scored 0.680 against 0.684; public A/Bs: ±0.004 per LoRA member | No gain inside the stack. Rebuilding the stack ourselves reaches about its level at best |
| Sibling-focused training, family gates | Exp 11: −0.008; public gates: 0.559 → 0.559, 0.552 → 0.552 | No |
| Generative classifier (one LM per label) | Exp 7: 0.280 against 0.530 | No |
| Assignment inside test prompt groups | Ours: +0.000 at two answers per prompt; public: −0.009 to −0.017 on the board | No. The twin rule (+0.013 at two per prompt) is already in the stack |
| Evidence from the real open-weight models | Ceiling +0.13 on our 8B; public: +0.035 out of fold, +0.007 to +0.010 on the board for the GLM pair alone | **Yes: the one stage with a large measured ceiling.** K3 and a sharper GLM member are open |
| Official-like training rows for our own members | +0.018 per doubling on the 8B; top teams' own statements | **Yes, if the host's setup per label can be matched**; that is the hard part |

So no change to the classifier's architecture closes the gap. The stack gains from evidence the classifiers do not
have (the real models) and from rows that look like the host's.

## What 0.75 is probably made of

An estimate, not something I could verify: top teams do not publish before the deadline.

- A base at about the public stack's level or a little above, built from single models at 0.65-0.68 instead of
  0.62-0.65. Reported cause: their own generated data, plus 14B backbones and full fine-tuning.
- The real-model stages: GLM pair (public since 13 Nov) and Kimi K3 (slices public since 29 Oct, widely
  downloaded, used by at least the 12th-placed team).
- The twin rule and soft balancing, which the public stack also has.

Stronger singles (+0.03 to +0.04 on the base) plus real-model stages worth more than the public's first version
account for the 0.056 between 0.694 and 0.75. The team that reports few submissions and little external data fits
a design that leans on the real-model stages; I cannot tell more than that.

## Proposed architecture and order of work

One pipeline, three sources of evidence:

    test passages
      |- public stack, unchanged, re-forked as it advances            -> log-probabilities z
      |- real-model evidence on the rows with open-weight mass:
      |     GLM-5.3 + GLM-5.3-Flash (public), Kimi K3 (to add),
      |     token-level contrasts and a neutral reference             -> small fitted member
      |- our own member(s), trained on official + verified official-like rows
      `- blend on z, twin rule on inferred groups, inside an 8 h budget

| Step | What | Expected on the board | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Fork the 0.694 notebook as the new base (the builder pins inputs and checks cell hashes, as for 0.684) | 0.689-0.694 | Owner's OK to push and submit; one submission |
| 2 | A sharper GLM member, studied on CPU from the published train-side scores of both models: token-by-token Flash − GLM contrasts (`candidate_eval.py --pair`) and the neutral reference that doubled Flash's gain in Exp 10 (+0.008 → +0.016). The stack already mounts Qwen3-4B-Base, so the reference needs no new input | +0.00 to +0.01 over step 1 | OK to download the public kernel output's CSV / NPZ files (no binaries); then a board A/B |
| 3 | Official-like rows for our own members. First a three-point curve at a fixed epoch on TC2 (24 / 48 / 96 prompts, about 1 GPU-hour) to confirm the 8B's slope, since the 14B does not show it. Then match the host's settings label by label with the closeness instrument on TC2, starting where we hold verified access others lack: gemini-3.7-flash through Agent Platform until 28 Jan 2027 (the 3.7 ↔ 3.8 cell is our largest single confusion, 144 rows; the pool has 3,278 rows of 3.7, none verified) | +0.01 to +0.02 per retrained member set; more only if every member is retrained on it | TC2 slots (owner's OK per job); API spend |
| later | Kimi K3 stage: only if a public notebook shows its timing and a gain on the board. It needs 2 to 3 h that the pipeline does not have, so something of that size would have to be cut | unknown (ceiling +0.04 on our 8B; my earlier +0.005 to +0.015 was a guess) | 2-3 h of the 9 h limit |

Without step 3 the realistic range is 0.70-0.71. Reaching 0.75 needs step 3 to work and the members retrained on
its data, which is the top teams' own recipe and weeks of TC2 time on one L40S.
